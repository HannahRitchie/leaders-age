How old are the world's leaders, and how long have they led?

Each bar starts at the leader's age when they took office and ends at their age today, so its length is their time in office. Based on the person who holds main executive power: the prime minister, president, or monarch.

Notes, sources and caveats

Who counts as the leader

The person who holds the main executive power: the prime minister in most parliamentary systems, the president in presidential and semi-presidential ones, and the monarch in Gulf and other executive monarchies. For Saudi Arabia that is Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (prime minister since 2022); King Salman, born 1935, remains head of state. Xi and Tô Lâm are counted from taking the top party post. Many ceremonial presidents and kings are not shown.

Terms

Putin's 2008–2012 spell as prime minister appears as a gap. Erdoğan is one bar from 2003 (prime minister, then president from 2014), and Togo's Faure Gnassingbé likewise runs from 2005. Where a leader went straight from one office to another, the bar is continuous.

Accuracy

Every birth date was checked against two independent sources (English Wikipedia and Wikidata), and every term start and end date against Wikipedia. That check corrected a number of dates from my first draft. Where the two sources disagreed I looked further.

Two leaders have an official birth date that differs from other published ones, and I used the official one: Lee Jae-myung (22 December 1964, although other sources say about 8 December 1963) and Tarique Rahman (1968 on his national ID card; other sources say 1965 or 1967). For Mauritania's Ghazouani, Wikipedia and Wikidata differ by 27 days (4 or 31 December 1956) and I used the 4th. For acting or interim leaders (Tokayev, Japarov, Mirziyoyev, Delcy Rodríguez) the clock starts the day they took power.

Sweden's Kristersson is a caretaker following the September 2026 election, and Brazil's Lula stays until January 2027.

Not included

Countries are left out where I could not establish the leader's birth date reliably. Credible sources disagree on the birth year of North Korea's Kim Jong Un, Oman's Sultan Haitham and Guinea's Mamady Doumbouya. I also left out Afghanistan, Benin, Botswana, Bulgaria, Burundi, Cape Verde, Colombia, Georgia, Grenada, Guinea-Bissau, Haiti, Iraq, Latvia, Lesotho, Libya, Lithuania, Madagascar, Mali, Mongolia, Niger, North Macedonia, Serbia, Seychelles, Suriname, Switzerland, Uruguay, Yemen, and most Pacific and Caribbean microstates.
