How to read it

For each region, the line is the span between the youngest and the oldest current leader, and the large dot is the median: half of the region's leaders are younger and half older. Hover or tap a dot to see which leader it is.

Regions

Russia is counted in Europe and Turkey in Asia. North America includes Central America and the Caribbean, and Oceania is Australia, New Zealand, Fiji and Papua New Guinea, so its figures rest on four countries. The G20 row covers the 19 member countries (the European Union and African Union are not included).

Political regimes

Regimes use the Regimes of the World classification (Lührmann, Tannenberg and Lindberg, 2018), as scored by V-Dem for 2025 (Democracy Report v16, March 2026), via Our World in Data. In a liberal democracy, there are free and fair multiparty elections plus individual and minority rights, and the courts and legislature constrain the executive. An electoral democracy has free and fair multiparty elections without those further protections. An electoral autocracy holds multiparty elections that are not free and fair. In a closed autocracy, citizens cannot choose the chief executive or the legislature through multiparty elections.

The classification describes 2025, while the leaders are those in office today. Where the government changed in 2026 (for example Hungary, Bangladesh, Nepal and Venezuela), the regime label reflects the previous government's year. Brunei, the Bahamas and Dominica are not classified by V-Dem and are left out of the regime view; the World and G20 comparison rows then use the same countries as the groups above them. Palestine uses V-Dem's score for the West Bank.

Who counts as the leader

The person who holds the main executive power: the prime minister in most parliamentary systems, the president in presidential and semi-presidential ones, and the monarch in Gulf and other executive monarchies. For Saudi Arabia that is Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman; King Salman, born 1935, remains head of state.

Accuracy and coverage

Every birth date was checked against two independent sources (English Wikipedia and Wikidata). The dataset covers 143 countries. Countries are left out where the leader's birth date could not be established reliably (including North Korea, Oman and Guinea), and those with no clear national leader. Two leaders use an official birth date that differs from other published ones: Lee Jae-myung and Tarique Rahman. The full list is in the main chart's notes.
